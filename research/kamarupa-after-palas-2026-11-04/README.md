# Kamarupa after the Palas: evidence audit

Research and map revision: 4 November 2026. Covers the early twelfth century to the Kamata transition around 1257–1260. [Events CSV](events.csv), [source register](sources.csv), and [live map data](../../data/kamarupa-after-palas.json).

The existing catalogue supplied exact-looking reigns for undated rulers and placed Samudrapala around 1100. This revision replaces that certainty with dated attestations and clearly identified historical reconstructions. Display windows in the JSON and events CSV determine when the explanatory context is shown; they are **not individual reigns or ownership intervals**.

| Date / period | Revised account |
|---|---|
| Early twelfth century | The end of Jayapala's line and Ramapala's intervention cannot be converted into a securely dated unified successor kingdom. Tingyadeva's rule and disaffection are known retrospectively through the Kamauli narrative. |
| First half of twelfth century | Kumarapala appoints Vaidyadeva to replace Tingyadeva. The grant is issued from Hamsakonci and concerns Kamarupa-mandala in Pragjyotisa-bhukti. Regnal year 4 is firmer than its disputed CE conversion. The original editor preferred 1142; 1123 is another proposed conversion. |
| Twelfth century, individual reigns unknown | Bhaskara → Rayarideva-Trailokyasimha → Udayakarna-Nihsankasimha → Vallabhadeva-Srivallabha is a genealogy. It does not establish four neatly divided reigns or a fully delineated north-bank kingdom. |
| Saka 1107, conventionally 1185 | Vallabhadeva's Assam plates record a charitable food-distribution establishment near a Siva temple east of Kirtipur in Hapyacha-mandala, at his father's command. The editor allows 1184/1185; the father should not automatically be assumed dead at the son's accession. |
| 1206 | Bakhtiyar Khalji's Tibet expedition and return are disastrous. The Kanai-Barashi-Bowa record commemorates the destruction of invading Turks, without naming the defender. Prithu's identification, genealogy, supposed 1187–1228 reign and Bhitargarh capital are not treated as established. |
| c.1226–1227 | Iwaz's eastern campaigns and tribute claims belong to this period. Scholarship reads the Gachtal stone record as Saka 1149/1227 and associates it with Visvasundara and temple restoration. Identification with Prithu remains disputed. Iwaz's reign conventionally ends in 1227, not 1228. |
| 1232 | Samudrapala is attested by the Ambari inscription, Saka 1154. His ancestry and reign limits are unknown. The record's Yogihati establishment must not be equated automatically with its Ambari findspot or with a later Sankaradeva sattra. |
| c.1250–1270 | Sandhya is associated with the Rai of Kamrup and the western reorganisation. This is a reconstructed reign; no dated accession in 1250 is asserted. |
| 1257; probably after 1257 / around 1260 | Yuzbak's invasion ends in defeat and death; historians associate the resistance with Sandhya. The capital shift toward Kamatapur is not securely dated to the same year. The atlas retains 1257 as a conventional map handover and explicitly labels that convention. |

The principal primary editions are [Kamauli, EI II, pp.347–358](https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_02/032556/407) and [Vallabhadeva, EI V, pp.181–188](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_05/032559). The original editions themselves explain the dating and geographic uncertainty. The Gachtal reading and Samudrapala's date were checked through scholarship and official inventories; the full stone texts were not freshly collated. This limitation is preserved in the map's source notes.

## Spots and what they represent

- **Ambari:** ASI gives 26°11′8″N, 91°45′21″E. This marks an archaeological/inscription locality, not the demonstrated location of Yogihati. [ASI excavation account](https://www.asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/excavation8.html).
- **Kanai-Barashi-Bowa:** the published regional rock-art coordinate is 26°12′36.5″N, 91°44′31.4″E. This marks the inscription site, not an excavated battlefield. [Ancient Asia 15 (2024), locality table](https://ancient-asia-journal.com/upload/1/volume/Vol.%2015%20%282024%29/PDF/3_Jiangampou.pdf); [IGNCA report](https://ignca.gov.in/rockart_2015/Rockart_AssamReport2015.pdf).
- **Gachtal:** retained catalogue coordinate near Dabaka, explicitly approximate. The official inventory confirms a thirteenth-century stone inscription; it is distinct from the earlier Gachtal copper plates. [Assam Directorate of Archaeology](https://archaeology.assam.gov.in/information-services/detail/list-of-protected-archaeological-sites-and-monuments).
- **Tezpur:** a regional marker for the reported Vallabhadeva plates provenance. It is not asserted to be the capital or the exact grant village. Kirtipur and Hapyacha remain unlocated in this revision.
- **Kamarupanagara:** North Guwahati is the traditional regional identification. The existing approximate point is retained; no exact medieval palace is claimed.
- **Kamatapur / Gosanimari–Rajpat:** the older point was too far south. The updated point uses ASI's 26°09′N, 89°21′12″E locality. This archaeological identification does not establish Sandhya's moving date or prove every surviving structure belongs to his reign. [ASI Rajpat report](https://www.asikolkata.in/Publications_files/excavation_rajpat-98-2k.pdf).
- **Hamsakonci:** no point invented. Kamauli near Varanasi is the discovery locality of Vaidyadeva's plates, not his Assam headquarters.

## Changes to the map

Pitha labels, their red river overlay, the Ratnapitha chip and the ancient regional ownership scenario stop before 1100. Actual modern-reference rivers remain in their usual style. The period from 1100 to 1270 has its own evidence panel, dated context navigation, source links and site markers. No battle route or exact unrecorded frontier is invented. The background successor polygon remains explicitly schematic, representing several powers rather than a single continuously ruled state.

The old personal-date fields for the post-Pala catalogue have been cleared where a precise reign was not established. The new panel supplies the named rulers and attestations without forcing them into a fabricated succession. Exact boundaries of the successor powers, a fresh full stone-text collation, the CE conversion of Kamauli and the capital-moving date remain open research questions.
