# Koch formation and staged enlargement — 4 November 2026

The old map assigned the full Karatoya–Barnadi envelope to 1515. This revision starts with a small Chikna foothill region and enlarges the displayed kingdom in stages. These intermediate outlines are editorial reconstructions, not recovered annual frontier surveys.

## Research finding

University of North Bengal research describes Biswa Singha’s inherited chiefdom, successive campaigns against neighbouring chiefs, and later enlargement to the Karatoya–Barnadi domain. It locates Chikna in the western Assam foothills and discusses traditions of movement through Hingulabas toward the plains. Its chronology includes a c.1515 rise followed by roughly twelve years of consolidation and a c.1527 kingship tradition. These differ from the common 1515 coronation narrative. Capital-transfer years are unresolved. The map retains 1515 as the conventional dynastic start, rather than treating that year as completion of every conquest. [Foundation chapter, printed pp.58–62](https://ir.nbu.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/a6791edc-d3e6-49d0-a83d-2ab4dc27f98d/content).

Acharyya likewise describes neighbouring-chief campaigns as a sequence, not a single acquisition. His account supports the direction of the reconstruction, without supplying dated intermediate borders. [N. N. Acharyya, *History of Medieval Assam* (1966), p.189 onward](https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Indian%20Subcontinent/1966%20The%20History%20of%20Medieval%20Assam%20from%20the%2013th%20to%20the%2017th%20century%20by%20Acharya%20s.pdf).

The Naranarayan chapter distinguishes tributary submission from annexation and discusses military reverses. [University of North Bengal, chapter II](https://ir.nbu.ac.in/server/api/core/bitstreams/4761b814-9600-4b41-8532-358adf761c63/content). The first eastern campaign, the 1547 Pichala defeat, and the renewed 1562–1563 advance are cross-checked with the project’s Gait chronicle synthesis. The Koch campaign zone is therefore a temporary hatched overlay; it does not absorb the Dimasa, Jaintia, Manipur, Tripura or Sylhet kingdoms into settled Koch territory.

## Adopted map stages

| Display years | Visible geography | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1515–1519 | Small Saralbhanga-side Chikna foothill envelope | Conventional start; schematic locality and limits |
| 1520–1526 | Foothill base plus Sankosh–Manas plains sector | Local consolidation; 1520 is an editorial anchor |
| 1527–1532 | Western Kamata/Duars toward Karatoya | Intermediate western enlargement; 1527 is an approximate chronology anchor |
| 1533–1539 | Mature Karatoya–Barnadi envelope | Later Biswa Singha context; exact conquest timing unresolved |
| 1540–1545 | Inherited western core | Accession does not create a larger kingdom instantly |
| 1546–1547 | Core plus hatched north-bank advance | Temporary campaigning toward Narayanpur |
| 1548–1561 | Western core; eastern hatching removed | Pichala setback followed by preparation |
| 1562 | Core plus hatched eastern reach | Renewed Chilarai invasion |
| 1563–1580 | Existing post-Majuli bank-specific envelope | Approximate settlement interpretation; subsequent overlordship contested |

The 1520, 1527 and 1533 geometries are inferred display stages. They do not assert that Manas was an independently established Koch frontier in those years. No particular chief’s uncertain historical place-name has received a modern namesake pin. Surviving western Bhuyan sectors remain visible into the consolidation period, rather than disappearing wholesale in 1515. Their removal at 1533 is also an approximate display anchor.

The founding-capital marker is explicitly “Chikna region ≈”, a regional reference at 26.73N, 90.35E, not a surveyed palace. The later Kamatapur reference replaces the previous Cooch Behar pin from the first year of the dynasty. Its 1527 start is an editorial timing convention. Hingulabas is discussed without an invented exact move year or a falsely precise additional pin.
